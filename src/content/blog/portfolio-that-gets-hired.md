# Building a Portfolio That Actually Gets You Hired

After reviewing hundreds of developer portfolios (and building a few of my own), I've noticed patterns. Here's what actually matters.

## What Recruiters Look For (In Order)

1. **Can I see your code?** — GitHub links are essential
2. **Does it work?** — Live demos beat screenshots
3. **Is it relevant?** — Projects matching the job description
4. **Is it polished?** — Attention to detail signals professionalism

## The 3-Project Rule

You don't need 50 projects. You need 3 great ones:

1. **A complex app** — Shows you can handle real-world complexity
2. **A team project** — Proves you can collaborate (even open source counts)
3. **Something unique** — Demonstrates creativity and initiative

## Common Mistakes

### Too Much Focus on Design
Your portfolio doesn't need to win design awards. It needs to be:
- Fast
- Accessible
- Easy to navigate

### No Call to Action
Every page should guide visitors to either:
- View your work
- Read your content
- Contact you

### Missing Real-World Context
For each project, answer:
- What problem did it solve?
- What was your role?
- What did you learn?

## The Stack Doesn't Matter (Much)

I've seen developers get hired with portfolios built in:
- Plain HTML/CSS
- WordPress
- Next.js
- Even Notion

What matters is the content and presentation, not the technology.

## My Recommendation

If you're building a portfolio today:
1. Start with Next.js or Astro
2. Focus on 3 polished projects
3. Write at least 2-3 blog posts showing your thinking
4. Make sure mobile works perfectly
5. Add a clear way to contact you

Your portfolio is your personal pitch deck. Make every element intentional.
